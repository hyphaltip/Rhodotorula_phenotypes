# Idea 1 — Universality of the arrest: scaling-collapse of colony growth curves and a quenched-disorder phase diagram across Cu (AMBITIOUS)

## Persona
**Statistical Physicist** — I read the existing extent/t50 analysis and see it stops exactly where a physicist would start: it fitted two scalar summaries (t50, saturation fraction) of curves. The *shape* of the approach to saturation, whether all 320 colonies can be made to collapse onto one master curve under a Cu-dependent rescaling, and how the colony-to-colony spread behaves near that saturation, are untouched. To me the Cu dose is a **quenched disorder field** and each colony is a growing cluster in an inhomogeneous medium — colony growth is a surface-growth/interface-pinning problem (KPZ/random-pinning tacit), and Cu is tuning its effective disorder strength.

## Motivation
The existing growth-rate finding is essentially a warning: "the peak-slope-vs-Cu rise is a phase artifact." That framing invites the *phase* question head-on: the saturation fraction φ drops from 95.4% → 73.9% and t50 moves 72.6 → 64.1 h as Cu goes 0→30 mM. That is a smooth crossover, not a sharp transition — but crossovers are physics too. Statistically-physics thinking adds three moves on top of the existing extent/t50 analysis, none of which re-proposes it:
1. **Data collapse**: rescale each colony growth curve by its own (t50, τ, Asat) and ask whether every strain × Cu curve falls on a single master function f[(t − t50)/τ]. Collapse across *strains* for fixed Cu = universal growth law (genus-level physics); collapse across *Cu* = the field acts only through rescaled parameters (a renormalization statement, Gibrat-style).
2. **Arrest exponent**: quantify the *shape of the arrest* — is 1 − A(t)/Asat a stretched exponential, or a power law ∝ (t∞ − t)^β, telling us whether the growth front is exponentially pinned (thermal-like) or jammed (power-law, jamming/critical-pinning-like)? This is strictly beyond anything in the extent/t50 analysis.
3. **Disorder-roughening along the Gibrat axis**: does the *spread* of final colony sizes across replicate wells (CV/width of the lognormal fit) widen with Cu? Widening = the disorder field magnifies quenched heterogeneity in growth, i.e., Cu "roughens" the population — a measurable emergent property with industrial relevance.

## Connection to Existing Data
- `v_phenotype` gives per colony-object, per timepoint: `Shape_Area`, `hours_since_plate_start` (0–117 h, 236 distinct times), `copper_mm` (0–30, 7 levels), `well_position` (96/plate), `strain_id`, `replicate_label`, `run_number`. ~211,800 rows, 320 strains, 16 replicate plates per Cu.
- The growth-rates analysis already established the *honest* kinetic ground truth I build on (never re-deriving it): exponential doubling time ~flat at ~37 h, t50 72.6→64.1 h, saturation fraction 95.4→73.9 %. My contribution is entirely in the *post-threshold* regime (t > t50), which that analysis deliberately did not shape-resolve.
- Per-strain replicate structure (16 plates/Cu) is exactly what a collapse/resampling study needs: replicate colonies = internal bootstrap ensemble for the collapse residual.

## Approach
1. **Extract curves.** `SELECT strain_id, well_position, copper_mm, hours_since_plate_start, Shape_Area FROM v_phenotype WHERE strain_id IS NOT NULL` (plus `run_number`, `replicate_label` for blocking). Build per colony (strain × well × Cu) the time-course A(t). Drop colonies with < 30 timepoints; require ≥ 3 replicate wells per strain × Cu.
2. **Per-colony normalization.** Define A_sat,i = mean(Shape_Area) over the late window (90–110 h) for colony i (mirroring the existing control window but over all Cu). Define x_i(t) = A_i(t)/A_sat,i ∈ [0,1]. Estimate t50,i (time where x crosses 0.5, interpolation) and an arrest time-scale τ_i from the local slope at t50 (|dx/dt| half-width).
3. **Master-curve collapse (universality test).** For each Cu, search over τ_shift such that all strains' x_i vs (t − t50,i)/τ weigh onto one curve: minimize pooled RMS of pairwise deviation. Report the residual per Cu. A residual jump pinned at some Cu_c would signal a *universality-breaking* crossover — the most exciting possible outcome (a strain-dependent physics switch, not just parameter shift).
4. **Arrest-shape exponent.** In the tail t > t50: regress log(1 − x) on (t∞ − t) for power law vs on (t − t50) for stretched-exponential log-log. Report per strain × Cu the arrest exponent β (or stretched parameter ρ). Compare β's Cu-dependence across the three genera. Bootstrap over wells for error bars; report the exponent with 95% CI (not just point estimates — robust-analysis convention).
5. **Order-parameter phase diagram.** φ_species(Cu) = mean over strains of A_sat/A_sat|Cu=0 by species; fit φ = A(Cu_c − Cu)^β* near the top Cu levels and test whether all three genera share one (Cu_c, β*) = universal response to the Cu field, or split into distinct families (R. mucilaginosa vs kratochvilovae/araucariae already differ on the log2 sensitivity index — do they also differ on *how* saturation is approached?).
6. **Gibrat/roughening axis.** Per strain × Cu, fit log A_sat over replicate wells to a Gaussian; report sd(log A_sat) and the 10–90% width vs Cu. Test whether sd(log A_sat) vs Cu is flat (disorder decoupled from growth) or rising (Cu roughens the ensemble — quenched-disorder magnifies inter-colony spread). Contrast with the Cu-invariant doubling time: a growing *dispersion* with constant *mean rate* is signature "annealed vs quenched" disorder.

## Expected Insights
- Whether there exists a master arrest curve f(u) valid across strains and/or Cu — i.e., whether colony growth in a Cu field is *scaling-universal* (all curves differ only by (t50, τ, Asat)) or whether the shape itself changes. A clean collapse for R. mucilaginosa but failure for the sensitive kratochvilovae would be a *qualitative* statement no scalar index can make.
- The arrest exponent β: power-law (β ≈ 0.5–1) vs exponential-vs-time distinguishes "jammed, cooperative arrest" from "single-rate exponential" growth attenuation — a mechanistic candidate (nutrient/oxygen-front pinning vs metabolic stall) with production-relevant meaning (how steep the *harvest window* is at high Cu).
- Whether population dispersion (sd(log A_sat)) grows with Cu. Even at fixed mean rate, widened spread = higher run-to-run yield variance at industrial Cu levels — a directly actionable number, not a curve.

## Feasibility
- **Effort**: High
- **Data ready**: Yes — `v_phenotype` is exactly the grain needed (colony × time × Cu); no new data. Only preprocessing is reshaping to per-colony curves.
- **Methods available**: Standard — collapse optimization + weighted nonlinear regression + bootstrap; all implementable in R (`nls`/`optim`) or Julia/Python against the DuckDB view; no custom numerics.
- **Key risk**: The dominant kill risk is *measurement/segmentation noise at saturation* (confluent colonies merge; late-time Shape_Area may saturate from contact not biology, capping all curves into an artificial common asymptote that trivially "collapses"). Mitigation: restrict collapse to sub-confluent colonies (filter on `Shape_Extent`/`Shape_Solidity` so only isolated objects), and check sensitivity of β to the A_sat definition window. Second risk: only 32 timepoints beyond t50 in the sparsest runs → weak tail resolution for β (mitigate by joining strains within species to pool the tail).

# Idea 2 — Roughness & the ordering transition: intra-colony fluctuation exponents and edge-vs-core asymmetry as early, label-free Cu/strain discriminators (TRACTABLE)

## Persona
**Statistical Physicist** — In phase-transition physics the *order parameter's own fluctuations* (susceptibility, correlation length) are more informative than its mean; you only believe an ordering transition when the fluctuations spike or collapse at the onset. The color data in this screen are perfect fluctuation observables and are almost entirely unused: per-colony within-pixel histograms are reported as Q1/Median/Q3/StdDev/CoeffVar, i.e., I already have a *coarse-grained local susceptibility per colony and per timepoint* without doing a single pixel of new image work. My physics add is: treat intra-colony spread as a fluctuating order parameter, its Q1–Q3 width as a correlation-length proxy, and the time-course of chroma as a *pigment-ordering kinetic transition* — extract its onset time (not endpoint medians already done) and compare it to the growth arrest.

## Motivation
The existing analyses consumed only per-colony *location* statistics (Median of L*/a*/b*, Shape_Area) and one aggregate CV proxy in the plate-position study (L* within-plate variance ~0.1–4.7%). The ~170 unused columns include the *within-colony* heterogeneity statistics for every color channel (ColorLab L*/a*/b* {Q1,Q3,StdDev,CoeffVar}, Colorxy, ColorHSV with its circular-Hue trap, ChromaEstimatedMedian/Mean) plus 65 TextureGray Haralick features (with `-avg-scale05` angle-averaged variants) and shape-roughness descriptors (Shape_Compactness, Shape_Circularity, Shape_Extent, Shape_Solidity, Max/Mean/MedianRadius). Physically, a growing pigment-filled colony should show a **late-stage "condensation"**: chroma rises, the pixel histogram narrows (CV collapses), and the edge–core gradient sharpens (Q3−Q1 grows) as a mature, ordered carotenoid core forms. The **onset time of that ordering, and whether Cu delays or abolishes it, is a new, kinetics-level phenotype** orthogonal to the extent-based t50 that the growth analysis measured. It is also production-relevant: BRET/carotenoid yield is about *when pigment saturates, not when area saturates*.

## Connection to Existing Data
- `v_phenotype` columns per colony × timepoint: `ColorLab_ChromaEstimatedMedian`, `ColorLab_ChromaEstimatedMean`, `ColorLab_L*Median/Q1/Q3/StdDev/CoeffVar`, `ColorLab_a*Median`, `ColorLab_b*Median`, `Colorxy_xMedian/yMedian`, `ColorHSV_*Median/StdDev`, `TextureGray_Contrast-avg-scale05`, `TextureGray_AngularSecondMoment-avg-scale05`, `TextureGray_Entropy-avg-scale05`, `Shape_Compactness`, `Shape_Circularity`, `Shape_Extent`, `Shape_Solidity`, `Shape_MaxRadius`, `Shape_MeanRadius`, plus time axis `hours_since_plate_start`, `well_position`, `copper_mm`, `strain_id`.
- The growth-rates work already gives me the comparator axis (t50 per condition); I'm *not* re-doing extent kinetics, only attaching a pigment-ordering time t_pigm.
- Genus/species labels and the existing log2 sensitivity ranking (25–30 vs 0–5 mM max area) give a prior for which species should be tested for *early* separation in roughness space (mucilaginosa tolerant vs kratochvilovae/araucariae sensitive).

## Approach
1. **Construct three fluctuation observables per colony-object at each timepoint** (pure SQL, no image re-processing):
   - **Core-edge asymmetry** ΔL_i(t) = `ColorLab_L*Q3` − `ColorLab_L*Q1` and, for chroma, ΔC = `ColorLab_ChromaEstimatedMedian` − `ColorLab_ChromaEstimatedMean` (a right-skewed pixel histogram ⇒ mean < median ⇒ chroma concentrated in a minority of core pixels — a literal "core" signature).
   - **Intra-colony relative width** CV_L = `ColorLab_L*CoeffVar` (the per-colony "susceptibility"), likewise CV_b, CV_Chroma if available; guard against division-by-small-mean.
   - **Interface roughness proxies**: `Shape_Compactness`, `Shape_Circularity`, `Shape_Extent`, and the ratio `Shape_MaxRadius`/`Shape_MeanRadius` (normalized interface excursion — a 1-D "surface height" observable), plus `TextureGray_Contrast-avg-scale05` and `TextureGray_Entropy-avg-scale05` (edge texture).
2. **Pigment-ordering onset t_pigm.** Per strain × Cu, on the control-plate and all-Cu time-series, do change-point detection on `ColorLab_ChromaEstimatedMedian` over `hours_since_plate_start` (piecewise-constant fit / CuSUM or PELT via the `changepoint` R package). Extract t_pigm where chroma exits baseline. Use `ColorHSV_HueMedian` with *circular* statistics (Hue is periodic — never arithmetic mean; use circular mean/CuSUM on sin/cos) so the onset is robust.
3. **Ordering-fluctuation collapse.** Within strain × Cu, plot CV_L and ΔC vs hours; check whether the CV collapses (fluctuation quench) at t_pigm — the physicist's "ordering transition shows a fluctuation dip." Compare onset lag τ = (t_pigm − t50) per strain × Cu; test whether Cu widens or destroys the ordering window (τ → ∞ = pigment never condenses = "Cu quenches the ordering transition").
4. **Early-separation test.** Restrict to early times (≤ 40 h, well before t50) and ask whether (CV_L, ΔC, roughness compactness) already separate the known-sensitive species from mucilaginosa at Cu = 0 — before any extent difference is visible. Report AUROC or a simple permutation test of the separation, to check the "roughness is an earlier readout than area" hypothesis.
5. **Robustness.** Block on `run_number`/`plate_number` (plate-position study showed plate variance is small but plate is nested in Cu). Recompute CV with `Intensity_MedianIntensity`-collinearity caveat in mind (L* ≈ Intensity >0.99): prefer a*/b* and chroma for the ordering observable, keep L* only as a check.

## Expected Insights
- Whether intra-colony fluctuation *width* (CV_L, ΔC) is a **better early discriminator than area** for the known-sensitive species — a direct, cheap, label-free screening axis usable at early imaging timepoints (industrial relevance: stop the screen at 40 h, not 110 h).
- Whether pigment ordering (t_pigm) and spatial arrest (t50) **track each other or decouple with Cu** — a decoupling (Cu kills growth order but not pigment order, or vice versa) is a genuine new finding linking carotenoid production to Cu stress, and it cannot be read off any endpoint-median table the existing analyses produced.
- A first map of **interface-roughness behaviors across strains/Cu** (smooth vs fractal edge via TextureGray Contrast / compactness over time) — purely descriptive data the project does not yet have in any summary.

## Feasibility
- **Effort**: Low–Medium
- **Data ready**: Yes — all observables are existing `v_phenotype` columns; the only new work is change-point + circular-stat handling. Zero image re-processing.
- **Methods available**: Standard — SQL aggregation + `changepoint`/PELT for onset, `circular` R package for Hue, permutation tests; all off the shelf.
- **Key risk**: Dominant risk is *segmentation artifacts masquerading as roughness* — partially merged/split colonies inflate Q3−Q1 and compactness late in the run (confluence again). Mitigation: filter to isolated objects via `Shape_Solidity`/`Shape_Extent` before computing ΔC/CV; the confluent-window result will be unreliable. Second risk: chroma mean-vs-median difference is small (σ noise) at low pigment levels for pale strains → the ΔC observable may be silent for weakly pigmented taxa; plan the ΔC test on the strongest pigmenters first (mucilaginosa) as a positive control.
